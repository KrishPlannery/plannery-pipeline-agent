# Nursegrid Financial Survey Insights
## Source: Nursegrid/Plannery Financial Survey, November 2024
## Respondents: 9,757 healthcare professionals
## Usage: Pipeline agent draws from these findings for Cold Week 1 emails and content

---

## HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Each insight below is written as a ready-to-use email hook. The agent selects the most relevant
finding based on the account's context — hospital size, workforce composition, last conversation
topic, and geographic market. Every finding is sourced directly from the survey data and is
citable as: "Nursegrid/Plannery survey of 9,757 healthcare professionals, November 2024."

Confidence level on all findings: High (n=9,757, statistically robust at every segment).

---

## TIER 1: HEADLINE FINDINGS
### Use these for any account regardless of context. Universally relevant.

**Finding 1: Three in four healthcare professionals are financially stressed**
- 75.0% of respondents agreed or strongly agreed: "I am generally stressed about my finances"
- Only 45.9% feel financially secure
- Email hook: *"We surveyed 9,757 healthcare professionals last year. Three in four told us they are generally stressed about their finances. That number held consistent regardless of whether they earned $60K or $110K."*

**Finding 2: Financial stress does not go away with higher pay**
- Financial stress among nurses earning $60K–$75K: 76.1%
- Financial stress among nurses earning $90K–$110K: 70.8%
- Even among nurses earning $135K+: 57.2% are financially stressed
- Email hook: *"One finding from our survey surprised us — nurses earning over $90,000 a year still report financial stress at a 70% rate. This is not an income problem. It is a debt structure problem."*

**Finding 3: Nearly 1 in 3 could not cover a $400 emergency without going into debt**
- 10.2% would not be able to pay for a $400 emergency at all
- 23.5% would put it on a credit card and pay it off over time
- 6.2% would borrow from a friend or family member
- Combined: 39.9% cannot cover a $400 emergency from cash or savings
- Email hook: *"Four in ten of the nurses we surveyed could not cover a $400 emergency expense from cash or savings. These are the same nurses working double shifts to keep your units staffed."*

**Finding 4: 70% cannot save enough for retirement**
- 69.9% disagreed or strongly disagreed: "I am able to save enough to meet my retirement goals"
- Email hook: *"Seven in ten healthcare professionals in our survey said they cannot save enough for retirement. That number is not driven by low earners — it shows up at nearly every pay band we measured."*

**Finding 5: 88.7% are interested in a financial solution**
- 48.3% highly interested in financial solutions customized for nurses
- 40.4% somewhat interested
- Combined: 88.7% interested
- Email hook: *"When we asked nurses if they would be interested in financial solutions built specifically for healthcare professionals, 88% said yes. The demand is there. The question is whether their employer makes it accessible."*

## TIER 2: WORKFORCE BEHAVIOR FINDINGS
### Use these when the conversation has touched on staffing pressure, extra shifts, or turnover.

**Finding 6: 60% of nurses pick up extra shifts as a financial strategy**
- 60.0% reported picking up extra shifts at their current job as a primary strategy to improve their finances
- 61.8% cut back on expenses
- 19.8% take a second job within nursing
- 19.9% earn income outside of nursing (gig work, side hustles)
- Email hook: *"In our survey, 60% of nurses said they pick up extra shifts primarily for financial reasons — not because they want to, but because they need the income. That dynamic has a direct relationship with burnout and turnover."*

**Finding 7: Extra shift pickup is highest among lower-paid nurses — your hardest-to-retain cohort**
- Nurses earning under $30K: 65.3% pick up extra shifts for financial reasons
- Nurses earning $30K–$45K: 69.0%
- Nurses earning $75K–$90K: 59.2%
- The financial pressure driving extra shift pickup does not go away even at higher pay bands
- Email hook: *"The nurses most likely to pick up extra shifts for financial reasons are also the nurses most likely to leave — your MAs, CNAs, and early-career RNs. In our survey, nearly 70% of nurses in that pay range reported picking up extra shifts as a financial strategy."*

**Finding 8: 20% of nurses have a second job or gig work**
- Nearly 1 in 5 nurses are supplementing their income with a second job or gig work
- Email hook: *"One in five nurses in our survey has a second job or earns gig income outside of nursing. That is time and energy that is not going into your patients or your units."*

## TIER 3: DEBT PROFILE FINDINGS
### Use these when the conversation has touched on financial wellness products, competing benefits, or debt.

**Finding 9: Two-thirds of nurses carry credit card debt**
- 66.2% of respondents carry credit card debt
- 58.4% carry student loan debt
- 27.8% carry personal loans
- Only 8.4% hold no debt at all
- Email hook: *"Two-thirds of the nurses in our survey carry credit card debt. Student loans get most of the attention in healthcare benefits, but credit card debt is the larger and more expensive problem — and it is the one almost no employer benefit touches."*

**Finding 10: 91.6% of nurses carry some form of debt**
- Only 8.4% hold no debt
- The average nurse in this survey carries multiple debt types simultaneously
- Email hook: *"In our survey of nearly 10,000 healthcare professionals, only 8% carried no debt. The other 92% are managing at least one debt obligation — often multiple simultaneously — on a healthcare salary."*

**Finding 11: 25% of nurses have credit scores below 650 — the traditional cutoff for prime lending**
- 25.0% have credit scores below 650
- 48.4% have credit scores below 720
- Plannery supports borrowers with credit scores as low as 580
- Email hook: *"A quarter of the nurses we surveyed have credit scores below 650 — which locks them out of most traditional financial products. They end up in high-interest debt with no clear path out. That is the gap Plannery was built to fill."*

**Finding 12: Rising everyday expenses are the #1 cited cause of financial stress**
- 70.1% cited rising everyday expenses (housing, groceries, utilities) as a driver
- 50.7% cited wages not keeping up with inflation
- 42.2% cited credit card or personal loan debt
- 40.1% cited inability to save for the future
- 37.2% cited student loans
- Email hook: *"When we asked nurses what was causing their financial stress, student loans came in fifth. Rising everyday expenses — housing, groceries, utilities — came in first at 70%. The financial wellness conversation in healthcare has been focused on the wrong problem."*

## TIER 4: DEMOGRAPHIC FINDINGS
### Use these when the account has a known young workforce, or when the conversation has touched on Gen Z retention.

**Finding 13: The youngest nurses are the most financially stressed — and the largest cohort**
- 18-24 year olds: 76.2% financially stressed (n=1,867)
- 25-29 year olds: 78.0% financially stressed — the highest stress rate of any age group (n=2,578)
- Together, 18-29 year olds represent 45.6% of all respondents
- Email hook: *"The nurses most likely to leave in their first two years are also the most financially stressed. In our survey, 78% of nurses aged 25-29 reported general financial stress — the highest rate of any age group. That is also the largest group in your pipeline."*

**Finding 14: Young nurses are financially stressed even when they earn decent salaries**
- Among 18-29 year olds: 77.3% financially stressed
- 64.0% cannot save for retirement
- 61.1% carry student loan debt
- 56.0% carry credit card debt
- 59.3% pick up extra shifts for financial reasons
- Email hook: *"Among nurses aged 18 to 29 in our survey — your newest hires — 77% are financially stressed, 64% cannot save for retirement, and 59% are picking up extra shifts just to make ends meet. These are not disengaged employees. They are financially trapped ones."*

**Finding 15: Financial stress is remarkably consistent across pay bands — it is structural, not a salary problem**
- At $60K–$75K: 76.1% stressed
- At $75K–$90K: 74.9% stressed
- At $90K–$110K: 70.8% stressed
- The pattern holds: doubling salary from $45K to $90K reduces financial stress by only ~11 percentage points
- Email hook: *"Raising nursing salaries helps — but it does not solve the financial stress problem. In our data, nurses earning $90,000 a year still report financial stress at a 70% rate. The issue is debt structure, not income level."*

## TIER 5: INTEREST AND DEMAND FINDINGS
### Use these when the account has raised skepticism about whether nurses would actually use a financial benefit.

**Finding 16: Interest in financial solutions is highest among the nurses who need it most**
- Nurses earning under $30K: 61.2% highly interested in a financial solution
- Nurses earning $30K–$45K: 58.5% highly interested
- Even nurses earning $75K–$90K: 45.5% highly interested
- Interest is consistently above 45% at every pay band measured
- Email hook: *"We asked nurses how interested they would be in financial solutions built for healthcare professionals. At every pay band, interest was above 45%. Among lower-paid nurses — your MAs and early-career CNAs — interest was above 60%."*

**Finding 17: Demand is broad, not niche**
- 88.7% interested (highly + somewhat) in nurse-specific financial solutions
- Only 11.3% uninterested
- Email hook: *"Nine in ten nurses in our survey said they would be interested in financial solutions customized for healthcare professionals. This is not a niche benefit. It is a mainstream demand that most employers are not meeting."*

## COLD SEQUENCE SELECTION GUIDE

Match the finding to the account's context:

| Account Situation | Best Finding to Lead With |
|---|---|
| Hospital focused on Gen Z retention | Finding 13 or 14 — young nurse stress profile |
| Hospital dealing with extra shift / agency labor costs | Finding 6 or 7 — extra shifts as financial strategy |
| Hospital that already has a student loan benefit | Finding 12 — rising expenses #1 cause, not student loans |
| Hospital skeptical nurses would use a financial benefit | Finding 16 or 17 — demand data |
| Hospital where last conversation was about salary increases | Finding 2 or 15 — stress persists at higher pay bands |
| Hospital with a large MA or support staff workforce | Finding 7 — lower-paid nurses pick up most extra shifts |
| General cold re-engagement, no specific context | Finding 1, 3, or 5 — universally compelling |
| Hospital where debt or credit was mentioned | Finding 9, 10, or 11 — debt profile |
