## Section 1: Market Reality

### Is This a Winnable Market?

Yes. But the specific reasons it is hard are frequently misdiagnosed, which causes sellers to apply the wrong solutions.

**What makes it hard:**
- Hospital benefits sales cycles are 6–18 months, structural and not compressible regardless of product quality
- Pipeline can grow rapidly; closes cannot
- Pipeline fallthrough — deals going quiet for weeks or months — is revenue destruction, not a minor operational inconvenience. In a 6–18 month cycle, a deal that goes 8 weeks without contact has dramatically lower close probability than one touched every 2 weeks.
- HealthStream AEs have quota on HealthStream products. Plannery is a favor they do, not a number they make. Without deliberate enablement, they deprioritize within 60 days.

**What makes it winnable:**
- Plannery is already embedded in HealthStream — no new sales motion required, just an add-on to an existing conversation
- Nurse turnover is a board-level crisis at most hospital systems right now
- No cost, no integration, no IT lift eliminates the most common objections before they are raised
- NPS 86 and zero employee complaints across all activations — this is genuinely rare in the benefits category

Not all hospital HR contacts are equal. Selling to the wrong profile wastes months. Identify the profile before investing in the conversation.

## Section 2: The Buyer's Psychology

### The Core Insight — Read This First

> Hospital HR does not make decisions by maximizing upside. They make decisions by minimizing blame. Their job, functionally, is to not be responsible for bad outcomes. This is not a personal failing — it is a rational response to their actual incentive structure.

The asymmetry that governs every HR decision:

| Scenario | Career Outcome |
|---|---|
| New benefit causes 3 nurse complaints to the CNO | Career-damaging. Traceable. Remembered. |
| Benefit quietly improves retention by 8% | Attributed to "market conditions." Earns no one a promotion. |

**The implication for how you sell:**

Most B2B sales say: *"Here is the upside you gain."*

Plannery must say: *"Here is the downside risk you are already carrying that we eliminate."*

Upside claims produce polite interest. Downside elimination produces urgency.

---

### The One Exception: When HR Does Get Promoted

The phrase "no one gets promoted for bringing a new benefit" has one important exception directly relevant to Plannery.

Each 1% reduction in RN turnover saves the average hospital **$295,000** (NSI Nursing Solutions 2026 Retention Report). Nurse turnover is already a board-level conversation at most hospital systems. The CFO and CNO already care about it.

If HR can say *"I brought in a no-cost solution that reduced nursing turnover by X%"* — that is the one scenario in which an HR person does get promoted. The retention data does not just support the pitch. It changes the buyer's entire incentive structure.

### The Three Real Objections (Not the Stated Ones)

When HR says "we need to think about it" or "we already have a financial wellness solution," the actual objection is almost never what they said. There are three real objections underneath every stalled deal.

---

#### Real Objection 1: "If employees complain about this, it traces back to me"

The debt consolidation framing is the most dangerous surface area. To a risk-averse HR ear, "we help nurses consolidate debt" sounds like: *"We help nurses take on a new financial product they may later feel manipulated into — and when that happens, they will complain to HR."*

The fact that this is wrong does not matter. The perception is the obstacle.

**How to defuse it — use both, in this order:**

1. **Lead with NPS:** *"Our NPS among enrolled nurses is 86 — higher than most consumer apps nurses use daily."* This establishes that nurses love the product.
2. **Follow with zero complaints:** *"Across every hospital that has activated Plannery, we have had zero employee complaints reach HR."* This eliminates the specific fear.

Never swap the order. Never use one without the other. NPS answers "do nurses like it?" Zero complaints answers "will this come back to hurt me?" HR needs both answers.

---

#### Real Objection 2: "My job is already done. We check the financial wellness box."

HR bought a financial wellness product, got credit for it, and has zero incentive to revisit that decision. Telling them Plannery is better is an implicit accusation that they made a wrong call. They will not engage with that frame.

**How to defuse it:**

Do not compete. Do not compare. Say: *"Plannery is not a replacement — it is the piece your current solution does not touch. Most financial wellness platforms give employees education and budgeting tools. Plannery actually moves money, which is the only intervention that measurably changes financial stress levels in a way that shows up in attrition numbers."*

You are filling a gap, not asking them to fire a vendor.

---

#### Real Objection 3: "I don't know what happens after we turn this on"

No integration, no lift, turn it on in days — this sounds like a convenience pitch. But HR hears: *"We don't know what will happen to our employees once this is activated."* Ease of activation paradoxically triggers control anxiety. Process is how HR diffuses accountability.

**How to defuse it — reframe from convenience to liability elimination:**

*"We don't touch your systems. We don't require your IT team. We don't create a support ticket queue for your benefits staff. If something goes wrong on the employee side, it never reaches your desk."*

That is not a convenience pitch. That is a blame shield — which is what HR actually needs.

## Section 3: Framing That Works

### The Core Narrative

> *"Nurse turnover is your most expensive unsolved workforce problem. Plannery eliminates it quietly, at no cost, in days — and your clinical staff are already one click away inside HealthStream."*

Every piece of content, every email, every AE talking point derives from this. Notice what it does:
- Leads with the buyer's pain (turnover), not the product
- Eliminates the "is this worth my time" question immediately (no cost, no integration)
- Makes HealthStream the trust bridge — they already have a relationship
- Does not mention debt consolidation or AI coaching in the first sentence

---

### Broadening to Clinical Staff: The Right Way

Plannery is available to the entire workforce — clinical and non-clinical. When HR asks *"Is this available to everyone?"* the answer is yes, and that clears a compliance and fairness concern.

**Do not lead with "all employees."** General employee benefits go in the "we already check that box" bucket immediately.

Lead with the specific clinical role that is the presenting pain for that account. When in doubt, lead with nursing. If the AE has intel that the pain is MAs, APPs, or allied health, substitute that role. The financial stress mechanism and zero-cost no-integration proof points are identical across all segments.

| Clinical Segment | Turnover Profile | Plannery Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **RNs** | $60,090 per turnover; each 1% change = $295,000 (NSI 2026); most board-visible | Primary pitch anchor for most accounts |
| **Medical Assistants / Patient Care Techs** | High volume, lower per-head cost, but wages lowest relative to cost of living — financial stress most acute | Lead here when MA shortage is the presenting complaint |
| **Advanced Practice Providers (NPs, PAs)** | Lower turnover rate but $100k+ replacement cost including credentialing and panel rebuild | Lead here when APP retention is a strategic priority |
| **Allied Health (PT, OT, Rad Tech, Lab)** | Acute shortages, highly regional | Lead here for specialty-heavy systems |

### Framing Financial Stress Without Triggering Institutional Defensiveness

Some HR leaders will not engage publicly with a framing that implies their workforce has a financial problem — because it reflects on their compensation design.

**Attribute financial stress to systemic causes, not institutional ones:**

*"Nurses nationally carry significant student debt on healthcare salaries. This is a structural problem no hospital created and no single benefit fully solves — but Plannery is the one intervention that measurably reduces its impact on your retention numbers."*

That framing gives HR permission to engage without feeling implicated in the problem.

## Section 4: The Retention Data

### The Raw Number and Its Problem

Plannery-enrolled employees show meaningfully better retention than non-enrolled employees. Do not cite this raw figure with analytically literate hospital administrators.

**The objection that kills it:** *"Of course people who signed up for a financial wellness benefit are more financially stable and more likely to stay. You measured motivated employees, not Plannery's effect."*

This is a valid selection bias critique. Leading with the raw number actually reduces credibility rather than building it.

---

### What Makes the Data Credible

Plannery is building a rigorous outcomes analysis. Here is what that means in plain language:

**Propensity Score Matching:** We find a statistical twin for every Plannery borrower among the non-applicant population — matching on tenure, role, full/part-time status, and department. Then we compare retention between matched pairs. This neutralizes the selection bias objection.

**Three-Group Dose-Response:** We compare retention across three groups:
1. Applied and received a loan
2. Applied but did not qualify
3. Never applied

If retention is highest for Group 1, lower for Group 2, and lowest for Group 3, that pattern is a strong causal signal. It is very hard to explain away.

**Dollar Translation:** We apply NSI 2026 turnover cost data to translate retention lift into a dollar figure specific to each hospital's size.

**Methodological Transparency:** We disclose how we built the analysis. Almost no vendor does this. Transparency with a skeptical buyer is a trust signal, not a weakness.

---

### How to Use the Data in Conversation

1. Lead with the hospital's cost, not Plannery's performance: *"A hospital your size with typical RN attrition is likely spending $X million per year on nurse turnover. Here is how we calculate that."*
2. Introduce the matched analysis: *"We did not just compare borrowers to non-borrowers — we matched employees on tenure, role, and employment status first."*
3. Use the dose-response as the closer: *"Employees who received loans retained at higher rates than those who applied but did not qualify, who retained at higher rates than those who never applied. That pattern is very hard to explain away."*
4. Raise limitations yourself: *"We cannot control for every variable. But the dose-response pattern, combined with consistent results across multiple hospital systems, gives us high confidence the effect is real."*

| Input | Value |

| Conservative Plannery improvement | 2 percentage point reduction in RN turnover rate |
| Dollar value | 2 × $295,000 = $590,000 annually |
